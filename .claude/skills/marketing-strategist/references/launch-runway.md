# Channels & launch runway

Used in Phase 1 (channel selection) and Phase 4 (launch sequencing). This is the road from "audience" to "first sales."

## Channel selection — including the founder's "maybe social media?" question

Don't spread thin. In a small B2B legal-tech market, pick the few channels where the ICP actually is and the founder can be consistent. Recommended tiers:

**Core (owned + authority — where the real leverage is):**
- **Founder's LinkedIn.** For B2B legal-tech, this is *the* social channel that matters. It's where jurists, directors, and SME owners are reachable, and it compounds the founder's authority. Consistent native posts (education + build-in-public) > everything else social.
- **The journal / blog (owned).** Durable, searchable, shareable; the build-in-public engine.
- **Email list (owned).** The one audience no algorithm can take away. The nurture + launch backbone.
- **Offline workshops & talks.** The founder's superpower — warm, high-intent, pre-qualified. Every workshop should funnel attendees to the list. Turn a one-off talk into an ongoing relationship.
- **Professional communities** — legal/HR/SME associations, chambers of commerce, ABSL and similar. Contribute value, don't spam.

**Secondary (only if capacity allows):**
- Guest slots: podcasts, partner newsletters, association events — borrow other people's audiences.
- Referrals/word-of-mouth — engineer it deliberately; in a small market it's a top channel.

**Explicitly deprioritize (this answers the uncertainty):**
- **Instagram / TikTok / Facebook** as marketing channels: generally **not worth it** for this ICP and category — the buyers aren't there in a buying mindset, and the sober brand doesn't fit short-form entertainment. The *only* exception is if the founder personally wants a broader personal-brand play and has the time; even then, LinkedIn first.
- **Paid ads at scale:** not a primary engine in a market this size. Small **retargeting** of already-warm visitors *later* can be worth a modest test. Don't build the plan around paid.

**One-line rule:** LinkedIn + owned content + email + offline/community. Everything else is optional and earns its place only after these are consistent.

## The launch runway (Phase 4)

A product launch is a *sequence*, not a day. Phases (compress/expand to the founder's real timeline from discovery):

### Phase A — Foundation (now)
Positioning locked, site instrumented, journal live on a cadence, waitlist segmented, analytics running. You cannot launch on top of a leaky, un-instrumented funnel.

### Phase B — Audience building
Publish consistently (journal + LinkedIn), funnel every workshop into the list, contribute in communities. **Goal:** a target number of *qualified* segmented signups (set it in discovery). This is the slow-compounding phase; don't rush it.

### Phase C — Design partners
Recruit the handful of best-fit early users (see `funnel-and-leads.md` §5). Build *with* them, in public. **Goal:** real usage, feedback, and the first proof (quotes, a case study, logos). This de-risks the launch and creates the credibility the launch will lean on.

### Phase D — Warm-up / pre-launch (~2–4 weeks out)
- Tease the launch to the list and on LinkedIn — "early access opens soon."
- Build anticipation: countdown, a waitlist-only perk, "founding customer" framing.
- Collect explicit intent ("want in on day one? reply/register").
- Preview the offer and pricing signal so launch day converts warm demand, not cold curiosity.

### Phase E — Launch moment
A coordinated push, not a quiet toggle:
- **Email sequence** to the warmed list (announce → value/proof → open → urgency/close).
- **LinkedIn** launch posts from the founder (story + proof + CTA).
- **A live event/webinar** — reuse the founder's workshop muscle. A "Contracts under control, live" session is a perfect launch vehicle: teach, demo, open access at the end. Converts authority into sales in one motion.
- **Open access with a founding-customer offer** (see below).

### Phase F — Sales & iterate
Founder-led sales calls with the intent-signalers. Turn design partners into case studies and referrals. Iterate the offer based on real objections. Feed learnings back into the journal (build-in-public doesn't stop at launch).

## The launch offer

- **Founding-customer pricing/terms** — a real, time-boxed reason to act now (lower price locked in, or bonus onboarding, or shaping influence). 
- **Honest scarcity/urgency only** — limited founding cohort or a genuine deadline. Never fake countdowns; in a trust category, a caught bluff is fatal.
- **Match the ask to readiness** — a cold Crawl-stage prospect isn't ready to pay; route them to a lower-commitment step. A warm design-partner-adjacent lead gets the direct offer.

## Output
A dated **Launch Plan** in `marketing/strategy.md` (or `marketing/launch-plan.md`): the phased calendar with target dates and numeric goals per phase, the channel-by-channel play, the launch-week orchestration, and the offer. Pair it with `marketing/content-calendar.md` for the week-by-week publishing schedule.
